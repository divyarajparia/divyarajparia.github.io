Project

Tumor Segmentation via Synthetic Data

Overview

Ongoing research at USC's Laboratory for Machine Learning, Health, and Biomedicine, focused on automating tumor segmentation for breast and lung cancer. Manual annotation of medical images is time-consuming and requires significant clinical effort. Our goal is to build a segmentation model that generalizes across institutions and imaging conditions by addressing the domain shift problem — where models trained on one dataset fail when applied to another. We generate synthetic data to simulate diverse domain scenarios, helping the model learn more robust representations without requiring exhaustive real-world annotation.

Key Features
  • Synthetic data generation to simulate cross-domain imaging variability
  • Targets both breast and lung cancer tumor delineation
  • Reduces clinician annotation burden while maintaining segmentation accuracy
  • Designed for generalizability across real-world clinical workflows
  • Domain adaptation pipeline to handle scanner and protocol heterogeneity
